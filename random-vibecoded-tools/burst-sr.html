Burst stack

Shift, rotation and scale aligned frame accumulation, running live in the browser. Every incoming frame is matched against a running reference, warped into a shared sub-pixel grid, and blended in with a weight based on how well it agrees — indefinitely, not just over a fixed burst.

The pose carries the captured frame onto the reference. The x and y offset is the displacement at the image center, in input-video pixels (x right, y down). Rotation and scale pivot on that center: positive rotation turns the captured frame counter-clockwise on screen, and a scale above 1 enlarges the captured frame. Error is the mean absolute gray difference over the overlap, measured at 320 px wide with bilinear sampling, using the same metric for every row. Drag the preview to shift, use the arrow keys to shift, [ ] to rotate and , . to scale (Shift for ×10), or type values. Space captures, Enter commits, Esc discards, A applies the auto pose. The first capture becomes the reference.

Measure residual (key R) applies your current pose, then measures how far each ~128 px tile of the captured frame still sits from the reference, in input-video pixels. Arrows are drawn 30× longer than the real shift and point where the tile's content landed relative to the reference; green is under 0.15 px, amber under 0.30, red above, and gray dots are tiles without enough texture. "Varies" is the part that differs from tile to tile, which is what per-tile alignment could fix; "uniform part" is a single shared offset that a better global pose would remove. Apply the auto pose first to see what the global fit leaves behind. Moving the pose clears the measurement.

Alignment is a similarity transform: a shift, a rotation and a uniform scale about the image center, fitted to the whole frame. Each new frame is matched against the running average, first with a coarse integer-shift search, then with a three-level Gauss-Newton refinement of shift, rotation and scale at 320 px wide, and warped onto the output grid with GPU bilinear sampling. Perspective and parallax are not modeled. Per-pixel blend weight falls off with local color disagreement, so moving content is down-weighted rather than ghosted. If the match error spikes, the stack auto-resets rather than silently degrading.
